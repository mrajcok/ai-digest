Anthropic research · 2026-08-10

Learning more about Claude's mathematical capabilities

An unreleased research version of Claude has improved a longstanding lower bound for the fraction of zeros of the Riemann zeta function that satisfy the Riemann hypothesis, increasing it from 41.6% to 67.2%. This was achieved by combining results from prior work by Baluyot, Goldston, Suriajaya, Turnage-Butterbaugh, and Bombieri, using a novel approach involving quadratic forms and spaces of functions. While not resolving the Riemann hypothesis itself, Claude’s result extends existing mathematical research.

The model arrived at this finding after being prompted to tackle the Riemann hypothesis, initially generating 650 unsuccessful ideas. A subsequent session utilized 60 Claude subagents running 2,400 shell commands and writing hundreds of Python scripts over a day and a half, along with encouragement from a staff member. Claude independently verified its findings and produced both a research paper and a formally verifiable proof in Lean, validated by Anthropic mathematicians.

The process involved 31 million output tokens in Claude Code. This demonstrates AI’s potential to accelerate mathematical discovery, even in areas considered beyond current AI capabilities, and highlights the speed of progress in AI-driven research.

WeatherNext: AI model achieves breakthrough in forecasting cyclones

WeatherNext, an AI model from Google DeepMind and Google Research, achieves state-of-the-art accuracy in cyclone track, intensity, and wind structure forecasting, providing an average of 24 hours more lead time than previous models.

  • The model, WeatherNext Cyclones, leverages Functional Generative Networks (FGNs) to efficiently generate 1,000-member ensembles of predictions, capturing uncertainty and rare events.

AWS blog · 2026-08-07

Determining playoff clinching scenarios in the NHL using constraint programming

AWS Generative AI Innovation Center developed a system for automating NHL playoff clinching scenarios using constraint programming and tree search.

  • The system uses a constraint programming (CP) model, solved with Google OR-Tools CP-SAT solver, to determine if a team has already clinched based on current standings and remaining game outcomes.
  • A custom tree search, leveraging the CP model, identifies game outcomes over the next n days that would cause a team to clinch a playoff spot.
  • Preprocessing, pruning strategies, and node-ordering heuristics are employed to optimize the tree search’s efficiency, achieving runtimes in the order of minutes for 1-day scenarios.
  • Validation against four NHL seasons (2021-22 through 2024-25) confirmed 100% accuracy matching official NHL-published scenarios.
Mistral AI blog · 2026-08-04

Introducing Shieldstral.

Mistral AI has released Shieldstral, a 3 billion parameter open-weights model designed for multimodal safety classification. The core innovation is framing content moderation as a question-answering task, allowing users to define safety policies in plain language at inference time without requiring model retraining. Shieldstral accepts a structured input consisting of an instruction, a yes/no query representing the safety policy, and the content to be evaluated (text, image, or both), then outputs a calibrated safety score.

The model is designed to rival the performance of models up to seven times its size on text safety and achieves state-of-the-art results on multimodal moderation tasks. It was trained on a combination of real and synthetically generated data and requires only a single 16GB NVIDIA GPU for operation. Shieldstral is available under the Apache 2.0 license and was created utilizing Mistral’s Forge platform.

Key to Shieldstral's performance is its approach to data handling: unifying diverse datasets, teaching the model to discriminate between similar policies rather than memorizing labels, incorporating image data, and combining multiple fine-tuned checkpoints.

Mistral AI blog · 2026-07-08

Introducing Robostral Navigate

Mistral AI has introduced Robostral Navigate, an 8B parameter model designed for robot navigation using only a single RGB camera. Unlike many existing solutions that require depth sensors or multiple cameras, Navigate achieves a 76.6% success rate on unseen R2R-CE (Room-to-Room in Continuous Environments) benchmarks – surpassing existing single and multi-sensor approaches by 9.7 and 4.5 points respectively.

The model works by predicting target locations using "pointing" – inferring image coordinates and desired orientation – and falls back to metric displacements when the target is out of view. Training leveraged 2.4 million simulated trajectories across 350k scenes, using a token-efficient prefix-caching method that reduced training time by a factor of 22x.

Further performance gains (a 3.2% improvement in success rate) were achieved via online reinforcement learning with the CISPO algorithm. Robostral Navigate has demonstrated successful operation on various robot types—wheeled, legged, and flying—and is notable for being built entirely in-house, not relying on existing open-source visual-language models.

Peer review is overwhelmed—can it survive in the AI era?

Peer review is facing increasing strain due to a surge in publications, potentially impacting the quality and timeliness of research.
