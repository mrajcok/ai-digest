Anthropic research · 2026-11-16

Updating our coordinated vulnerability disclosure policy timelines

Anthropic is updating its coordinated vulnerability disclosure (CVD) policy timelines to reflect the increased speed of vulnerability discovery enabled by AI. Effective January 1, 2027, the disclosure window for high- and critical-severity issues will shorten from 90 to 30 days, and to 45 days for all other issues. This change acknowledges that AI models can both discover and potentially fix vulnerabilities more rapidly than traditional methods, reducing the likelihood of long-term privacy for identified flaws.

Alongside the shortened windows, Anthropic will escalate to an external vulnerability coordinator after 15 days of no response from maintainers—down from 30 days—and will wait 30 days, rather than 45, after a patch is available before releasing full technical details. Reports in progress before January 1, 2027, will retain their original deadlines. Anthropic is also providing a 30-day notice to projects with vulnerabilities reported over 90 days ago that exceed the current policy, intending to publish those reports by the start of the new policy. The seven-day disclosure window for actively exploited vulnerabilities remains unchanged; findings from tools like OSS Scanner, which lack human review, are excluded.

Anthropic research · 2026-11-13

Building strong safety evaluations

Anthropic outlines recommendations for building robust AI safety evaluations, emphasizing that a safety claim is only as strong as the evaluation supporting it. They advocate for assembling diverse teams – including both domain and methods experts – to design evaluations that measure harm propensity rather than just model capabilities. Crucially, evaluations should be run in isolated, monitored environments to prevent unintended actions.

The core of their approach focuses on designing evaluations to answer specific questions (observational vs. causal), accurately measuring the intended construct (like appropriate response rate in sensitive topics instead of simple refusal), and ensuring results generalize to real-world scenarios through multi-turn simulations that capture nuanced interactions. They stress the importance of rigorous grader validation against human labels, preregistering evaluation plans, and fully reporting statistical uncertainty.

Anthropic shares examples from their system cards and tools like Petri, highlighting a recent case where a system prompt update improved responses related to suicide and self-harm from 60% to 94% on claude.ai. Following these recommendations—measuring the right thing, validating graders, and reporting uncertainty—is key to trustworthy AI safety assessments.

AWS blog · 2026-11-16

Use prompt caching with open-weight models on Amazon Bedrock

Amazon Bedrock now supports prompt caching for open-weight models, reducing input costs and latency by reusing previously processed prompt context.

  • Prompt caching reuses the computed state of matching prompt prefixes, reading from the cache instead of reprocessing.
  • Two modes are available: implicit caching (automatic reuse of eligible prefixes) and explicit caching (reuse of content up to specified breakpoints).
  • Explicit caching allows for layered caching with multiple breakpoints, offering granular control over cached content.
  • The examples demonstrate using both implicit and explicit caching with the Chat Completions API, along with how to read cache usage details from the response, and utilizing implicit caching with the OpenCode coding agent.

AWS blog · 2026-11-16

Best practices for writing Denied Topics in Amazon Bedrock Guardrails

Amazon Bedrock Guardrails’ Denied Topics feature allows blocking content categories using plain language, but can unintentionally block legitimate queries if not implemented carefully.

  • The feature blocks content based on meaning, not keywords, but broad topic definitions can over-block, while narrow definitions may miss intended targets.
  • Effective topic creation requires focusing on the user’s intention rather than the subject domain, using action-oriented names (e.g., “Investment Recommendations” instead of “Finance”).
  • Topic definitions should begin with an action (e.g., “Asking for…”) and specify concrete objects to narrow the scope, avoiding broad subject-area openers.
  • A recommended structure for definitions is: “[Action or content-type opener] for [specific objects] by [qualifying constraint or purpose]”. Each topic has a name (max 100 characters), a definition (100-300 characters ideal), and up to five examples (max 100 characters each).
