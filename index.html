Anthropic blog · 2026-11-09

Which Claude model should you build with? Fable, Opus, Sonnet, or Haiku / claude.dev Blog

Anthropic has released four generally available Claude models: Haiku 5.5, Sonnet 5.5, Opus 5.5, and Fable 5.2, with Claude Mythos 5.2 available via verification programs. The recommendation is to begin most tasks with Opus 5.5 at medium effort, due to its balance of cost (half that of Fable 5.2) and capability.

For specific applications, Haiku 5.5 is best for simple, high-volume tasks like classification; Sonnet 5.5 suits well-scoped work such as bug fixes or everyday document creation; and Fable 5.2 excels in complex technical projects, and when Opus 5.5 at high effort is insufficient. Adjusting the “effort” level is encouraged as a granular way to tune cost and capability, and combining models—using smaller models as subagents for larger ones—can optimize workflows.

Considerations for model migration include compatibility checks, specifically regarding preserved thinking, tool use (especially the computer toolset), and safety fallbacks. The blog stresses the importance of measuring model performance on your own tasks with representative data and clear outcome checks to determine the most cost-effective and accurate solution.

Google research · 2026-11-10

Introducing AlphaProof Code: a breakthrough in verifying complex software at depth

AlphaProof Code, a new agentic framework from Google DeepMind, formally verified the code of the Signal protocol.

  • The system uses a multi-LLM agentic framework, powered by Gemini Carbon, to translate Signal’s security goals into formal mathematical specifications and then generate over six million lines of Lean code to prove the accuracy of the code.
  • AlphaProof Code successfully verified key security properties of the Signal protocol implementation – including protection during temporary device compromise – a feat previously unattained for a widely deployed cryptographic protocol.
  • The system identified and flagged approximately 1% of the codebase for human review, significantly reducing manual effort; it also correctly identified two reintroduced, previously fixed bugs.
  • The verification process completed in four weeks, compared to years for traditional methods, and the formal verification artifacts are being open-sourced.

Ars Technica news · 2026-11-10

Ukraine’s fiber-optic drones get squeezed by AI data center demand

Ukraine’s fiber-optic drone production is facing challenges due to increased demand and supply chain issues.

  • Both Russia and Ukraine are utilizing fiber-optic drones, which are resistant to jamming, but require skilled pilots and are more costly than traditional radio-controlled drones.
  • Demand for optical fiber has surged due to both drone warfare (estimated 50,000 drones/month between Russia and Ukraine) and the global AI data center buildout—TrendForce forecasts a 111 million mile shortfall in 2026.
  • China is the primary supplier of fiber-optic cable, with Russia importing significantly more (328,000 miles in August 2025) than Ukraine (72 miles in August 2025).
  • Ukrainian manufacturers like BlueBird Tech are adapting by bringing spooling processes in-house to improve quality control and reduce reliance on finished imports, despite rising costs (6-10x increases in 2025-2026).
