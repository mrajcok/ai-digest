Google research · 2026-09-30

Introducing SynthID Bio

SynthID Bio introduces a watermarking system for AI-generated biological data to enhance biosecurity and scientific integrity.

  • SynthID Bio embeds imperceptible signatures into protein sequences and 3D structures, preserving biological function, using adjustments to amino acid choices and atomic coordinates.
  • The system was validated with AlphaProteo and a SynthID-enabled ProteinMPNN, demonstrating comparable hit rates and binding affinities (KD measurements) to unwatermarked proteins for VEGF-A, SARS-CoV-2 spike protein RBD, and PD-L1.
  • For protein folding, SynthID Bio fine-tunes AlphaFold 3’s diffusion network for watermark integration, maintaining prediction accuracy and detectability even with digital noise.
  • The approach is being extended to watermark entire genomes using Evo 2, and code, weights, and in vitro data are being open-sourced to encourage community collaboration.

Google's early attempt to pay websites for AI answers is struggling

Google’s AI contribution pilot program, designed to compensate websites for content used in AI-powered search results, is reportedly yielding minimal payouts for most participants.

  • The pilot program, involving approximately 100 publishers, aims to pay websites when their content contributes to search results generated by Google’s Gemini AI.
  • Many small and mid-sized publishers report receiving payments equivalent to roughly one-tenth of one percent of their advertising revenue.
  • Early participants in niche areas like anime and gaming are seeing more substantial revenue (over $1 million annually for one), but recent additions report significantly lower earnings ($50,000 - $60,000).
  • Publishers have expressed confusion regarding how “meaningful contribution” is measured and are concerned about the program's ability to provide predictable revenue, leading some larger publishers to decline participation.
