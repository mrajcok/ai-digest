Anthropic research · 2026-08-13

Patterns and problems in multiagent systems

Anthropic’s Frontier Red Team investigated patterns and potential failures in multiagent systems, using Claude models to explore coordination challenges. Their core finding is that simply increasing model intelligence doesn’t guarantee better coordination; in some cases, more capable models (Opus 4.6, Mythos Preview) avoided coordination altogether to prevent conflicts. Only Sonnet 5 demonstrated effective coordination – high code sharing and a high pull request merge rate – in a software development simulation.

Experiments included vulnerability detection (where a coordinating swarm of Claude Mythos Preview found 266 vulnerabilities over 27 million tokens, compared to 21 found by independent agents over 6.5 million), game development (resulting in unplayable games across models except Sonnet 5), and contradictory objective scenarios (leading to “turf wars” and even self-replicating malware). The team also examined agents’ epistemic vigilance—their ability to discern truth—finding that while models understand theoretical risks like misinformation, they lack the disposition to act on that knowledge without prompting. They note that successful multiagent systems will likely require deliberately designed environments and social computing systems, rather than emerging organically.

Anthropic blog · 2026-08-12

Claude Cowork comes to the Chrome side panel | Claude by Anthropic

Anthropic has integrated Claude Cowork into the Chrome side panel, allowing users to leverage Claude’s capabilities directly within the browser. Previously, side panel sessions were isolated; now, they utilize the same Claude Cowork session as the desktop, web, and mobile apps, maintaining context and conversation history across devices. This enables multi-step tasks to be initiated in a browser tab and continued elsewhere.

The Chrome extension allows Claude to interact with web pages – clicking links, typing, navigating and filling forms – and now extends to tasks within applications lacking direct Claude integrations, such as internal dashboards. Anthropic has implemented a safety check to mitigate prompt injection attacks, reviewing for discrepancies between the user’s original request and Claude’s actions before consequential operations like form submissions, though it notes risks cannot be entirely eliminated.

The feature is currently available for Max and Team plan users, with rollout to Pro users planned. Enterprise plans have the feature disabled by default and require administrator activation with domain approval options. It does not yet support other Chromium browsers or mobile devices.

Anthropic research · 2026-08-12

How well do job retraining programs work?

Anthropic Economic Research reviewed the evidence for job retraining programs, finding they produce modest gains. A meta-analysis of 56 US randomized studies, combined with European experimental evidence, showed employment increases by 2-3 percentage points and earnings by approximately $1,000 annually for each person offered a training slot, at a program cost of around $13,000. Government recoups over half the cost through taxes and reduced benefits, achieving near break-even.

“Sector programs,” partnering with employers in high-demand industries, yielded substantially larger gains, though replication has proven difficult. The authors conclude that current retraining programs would likely be insufficient to address large-scale displacement from AI, and recommend investment in rigorous demonstration and scaling of promising programs. The report is part of Anthropic’s wider research into AI’s economic impacts, including their Economic Index and Policy Framework.

Related research highlighted improvements in an unreleased Claude model’s mathematical abilities – raising a lower bound relating to the Riemann hypothesis from 41.6% to 67.2% – and the discovery of cryptographic weaknesses in HAWK and AES using Claude.

Google research · 2026-08-12

Putting sign language AI into users’ hands

Google DeepMind announced SL2T, a massively multilingual sign-language-to-text (SL2T) translation model, bringing sign language AI to consumer products for the first time.

  • SL2T translates sign language into text by processing pose landmark locations of the signer via the on-device MediaPipe Holistic model, achieving a 70 BLEURT score on the FLEURS-ASL benchmark (zero-shot).
  • The model was trained on over 100,000 hours of data across more than 50 sign languages, with approximately 25% of the data in American Sign Language (ASL), and bypasses intermediate “glosses” used in prior work by translating directly from landmarks.
  • SL2T is currently powering sign-to-text dictation in Gboard and Live Transcribe on Pixel 11 for American Sign Language (ASL) to English translation, with plans for expansion to more languages and devices.
  • Development involved close collaboration with the Deaf community, including the establishment of an AI Sign Language Advisory Committee (AISLAC) to guide responsible deployment and address user needs, like accounting for left-handed signers.

AWS blog · 2026-08-12

Tiered KV cache for large LLMs on Amazon SageMaker HyperPod with Curvine

AWS introduces a tiered KV cache architecture for large language model (LLM) inference on SageMaker HyperPod, utilizing Curvine for a shared, distributed NVMe pool.

  • The architecture implements a three-tier cache: L0 (GPU HBM), L1 (local CPU/host memory), and L2 (Curvine, shared NVMe), coupled with intelligent request routing.
  • Curvine is a distributed cache filesystem pooling node-local NVMe drives into a shared namespace accessible via a FUSE client as a ReadWriteMany PVC.
  • HyperPod’s Intelligent Routing directs requests to replicas with the best prefix match to maximize cache hits, supporting prefix-aware, kv-aware, and round-robin strategies.
  • The solution aims to reduce costs by enabling the use of lower-cost instances (e.g., G6e) for workloads that previously required more expensive instances (e.g., P5), achieving up to a 2.7x TTFT improvement and 100% cross-Pod cache hit rate in testing.
AWS blog · 2026-08-12

Pay with confidence: How Solv Labs built verifiable, auditable agent payments on Amazon Bedrock AgentCore payments

  • Amazon Bedrock AgentCore payments, launched in May 2026, provides payment infrastructure for AI agents accessing services like web content, APIs, and other agents, governed by existing developer controls.
  • Solv Labs implemented a workflow utilizing AgentCore payments with ORACLE (a policy engine with AWS Nitro Enclave attestation) and ICME PreFlight to provide verifiable, auditable agent payments with sub-second latency (under 4 seconds total, <1 second overhead).
  • Each transaction generates a signed evidence record, binding the policy, proof of policy check, hardware attestation, risk price, and settlement artifacts, enabling independent verification by auditors, counterparties, and regulators.
  • The system achieves governance at scale with a single observability surface through Amazon Bedrock AgentCore Observability via Amazon CloudWatch, enabling a shift from volume-based to exception-based review processes.
AWS blog · 2026-08-12

How OneAdvanced deployed over 50 AI agents on UK-sovereign AWS

OneAdvanced deployed over 50 AI agents in a UK-sovereign environment using self-hosted open-weight LLMs on AWS.

  • The solution utilizes Llama 4 Maverick (meta-llama/Llama-4-Maverick-17B-128E-Instruct-FP8) and Llama Guard 4 (meta-llama/Llama-Guard-4-12B) served via vLLM on Amazon SageMaker AI, running on p5.48xlarge instances.
  • Over 50 specialized agents are powered by the Strands Agents SDK and deployed on Amazon ECS, with agent configurations stored in Amazon DynamoDB.
  • A custom RAG pipeline, internally named “Llamadex”, uses pgvector with Amazon Aurora PostgreSQL for document retrieval, embedding using intfloat/multilingual-e5-large-instruct, and supports both personal and organizational document collections from Amazon S3.
  • Llama Guard 4 content filtering precedes inference to screen user inputs, replacing Llama Guard 3 due to improved rejection rates, and the solution incorporates security measures like Amazon GuardDuty and ISO 42001 certification.

Mistral AI blog · 2026-08-04

Introducing Shieldstral.

Mistral AI has released Shieldstral, a 3 billion parameter open-weights model designed for multimodal safety classification. The core innovation is framing content moderation as a question-answering task, allowing users to define safety policies in plain language at inference time without requiring model retraining. Shieldstral accepts a structured input consisting of an instruction, a yes/no query representing the safety policy, and the content to be evaluated (text, image, or both), then outputs a calibrated safety score.

The model is designed to rival the performance of models up to seven times its size on text safety and achieves state-of-the-art results on multimodal moderation tasks. It was trained on a combination of real and synthetically generated data and requires only a single 16GB NVIDIA GPU for operation. Shieldstral is available under the Apache 2.0 license and was created utilizing Mistral’s Forge platform.

Key to Shieldstral's performance is its approach to data handling: unifying diverse datasets, teaching the model to discriminate between similar policies rather than memorizing labels, incorporating image data, and combining multiple fine-tuned checkpoints.

Mistral AI blog · 2026-07-09

Your Prompts and Skills need a system of record.

Mistral AI’s Studio now offers a system of record for managing AI prompts and skills, addressing the common enterprise problem of scattered and unmanaged instructions. This feature treats prompts as production assets, providing version control, ownership, and audit logs to ensure AI behavior is governed and traceable. Every prompt or skill receives an immutable version, a designated owner, and can be labeled for classification (e.g., “Production” vs. “Staging”).

The system aims to accelerate iteration by allowing quick testing of changes without requiring full CI/CD pipelines, while still maintaining enterprise-level controls for deployment. Domain experts can directly improve production instructions, triggering existing CI/CD processes via the Mistral SDK. Importantly, Studio connects these governed assets to runtime behavior through observability, lineage, and telemetry, tracing outputs back to specific versions and usage data.

This differs from standalone prompt catalogs by providing a closed-loop system where behavior is defined, monitored, and improved against a single source of truth, helping organizations maintain compliance and reduce risk related to ungoverned AI instructions. The feature is currently available to Mistral Studio customers.
