Anthropic Education Report: The AI Fluency Index · Claude Academy

Anthropic has released a report detailing the “AI Fluency Index,” quantifying how people effectively collaborate with AI. Using the 4D AI Fluency Framework—defining 24 behaviors, 11 of which are observable in conversations—they analyzed 9,830 anonymized Claude.ai conversations from January 2026. The study found that 85.7% of conversations involved “iteration and refinement,” repeatedly building on prior exchanges, and these conversations exhibited 2.67 additional fluency behaviors on average, including more questioning of Claude’s reasoning (5.6x more likely) and identification of missing context (4x more likely).

However, when users created artifacts—code, documents, etc.—they exhibited less critical evaluation, with a -3.1 percentage point change in questioning Claude’s reasoning and a -5.2pp change in identifying missing context. This suggests outputs perceived as ‘finished’ receive less scrutiny. The report highlights the importance of iterative conversation and critical assessment of AI outputs, and provides a baseline for tracking AI fluency as models evolve. The study acknowledges limitations relating to the sample population and the partial coverage of the framework.

Beyond the price per token: Choosing the right OpenAI model on Amazon Bedrock for your workload

Beyond the Price Per Token: Choosing the Right OpenAI Model on Amazon Bedrock

This post details benchmarking results comparing OpenAI models available on Amazon Bedrock (gpt-5.6-luna, gpt-5.6-terra, and gpt-5.6-sol) against cost-optimized OpenAI API models (gpt-5.4-mini and gpt-5.4-nano), emphasizing cost per successful outcome rather than solely price per token.

  • The benchmarking harness, openai-on-aws/benchmarks-openai, evaluates models using the OpenAI Responses API and assesses single-call accuracy (AIME, GPQA Diamond, MMLU-Pro), multi-turn agent trajectories (DeepSearchQA), and rubric-graded professional deliverables (GDPval).
  • Results indicate that after the July 2026 price reduction, gpt-5.6-luna on Amazon Bedrock often offers the lowest cost per correct answer, even compared to nano, despite nano's lower nominal token price.
  • Agent trajectory cost (influenced by turn count - more turns = more tokens sent) is a significant factor; Luna and Terra demonstrated higher F1 scores and fewer turns in DeepSearchQA than mini, resulting in lower costs per passing answer.
  • For professional deliverables evaluated with GDPval, Luna achieved the highest pass rate among cost-efficient models and the lowest cost per passing deliverable post-repricing, while Sol offers a different capability/price tier for genuinely difficult tasks needing higher accuracy.

Mistral AI blog · 2026-09-09

Modernizing complex legacy code with AI agents.

Mistral AI successfully migrated 40,000 lines of Fortran 77 code for a European energy operator to C++, addressing the challenge of maintaining and extending aging scientific codebases. A key aspect of their approach was establishing a “parity harness” – a system to verify numerical equivalence between the original Fortran and the translated C++ code, using exported state snapshots and a test framework. This ensured accuracy before extensive agent-driven translation began.

Initial attempts at fully autonomous translation produced syntactically correct but structurally poor code, essentially “Fortran retyped in C++.” Subsequent iterations employed structured agent workflows, combining AI “agents” with human oversight – a “planner,” “coder,” “tester,” and reviewer – focusing on manageable code modules (~10,000 lines). They also leveraged agents to document the legacy codebase by analyzing its call graph and incorporating information from existing PDFs using Mistral's OCR technology.

The project highlights three key principles for successful legacy code modernization: prioritize building a validation harness before coding, ensure comprehensive documentation, and implement structured workflows combining AI autonomy with human review gates. Approximately 40,000 lines were converted in the first sprint of a 300,000-line codebase.
