Agents you can coach: how Asana builds human-agent teams with Claude | Claude by Anthropic

Asana is integrating Claude models to create “AI teammates” that function within its existing Work Graph® structure — a model mapping tasks, projects, and relationships. Rather than building separate AI contexts, Asana’s approach treats agents as collaborators with defined roles, assigned tasks, and visible activity within the platform.

Employees can use Claude to structure their work by debating ideas and logging actionable items into projects and tasks, which agents can then execute. These agents have pre-built skills and integrations, with access scoped by administrators who control permissions, mirroring human user access controls but with an additional safeguard: an agent’s access is limited by the user who initiated the task.

A key feature is “shared memory,” allowing agents to retain information and improve over time. However, only designated administrators can commit feedback to permanent memory, while general user feedback is task-specific. Asana highlights three example applications: automating responses to product questions in Slack, briefing executives on at-risk customer renewals, and planning engineering cycles using Command by Asana, demonstrating Claude’s ability to handle document generation and complex tasks.

What do you want from AI?

Anthropic is launching a study using Anthropic Interviewer to gather user experiences with AI, aiming to inform the development of AI and guide policy. The study asks participants about their positive and negative experiences with AI, desired changes AI could bring to various aspects of life, and expectations for AI developers like Anthropic. Unlike a similar study from December 2025 involving 81,000 participants, this iteration allows users to opt-in to make their full interview publicly available for broader research and scrutiny.

The interviews, estimated to take around 15 minutes, are open to Claude and Claude Code users with accounts at least two weeks old, running from September 29 to October 6, 2026. Anthropic emphasizes transparency, noting the interviews will be published unedited (with limited exceptions for policy violations) and acknowledging the risks of re-identification despite not publishing account information. Participants are advised to avoid sharing personally identifying details during the interview.

This study builds on prior work which influenced Anthropic's research agenda and was presented at the World Economic Forum; however, it differs by providing a publicly accessible dataset of individual interviews, intending to foster wider research and public understanding of AI's impact.

Anthropic research · 2026-09-29

GLM-5.3 and the spread of advanced cyber capabilities

Zhipu AI’s GLM-5.3 demonstrates capabilities comparable to Anthropic’s Claude Mythos Preview in autonomously developing end-to-end cyber exploits. Testing on ExploitBench showed GLM-5.3 successfully created exploits in 50 of 410 attempts, mirroring the 56 of 410 successes achieved by Claude Mythos Preview. On a separate internal benchmark focusing on control-flow hijacks, GLM-5.3 succeeded in 4% of trials, slightly below Mythos Preview’s 6%, but a significant improvement over previous models like Claude Opus 4.6 and GLM-5.2 which achieved zero successes.

Crucially, GLM-5.3 differs from safeguarded US models by being released with easily bypassed safeguards; attackers can overcome these protections 64%-100% of the time using simple techniques like deceptive prompting or “abliteration” – a process of removing refusal mechanisms – while the same techniques fail against Claude models. Abliteration reduced GLM-5.3’s refusal rate to approximately 2-12% across several benchmarks and required roughly 2,200 GPU hours (around $4,400) to complete. NIST recently assessed GLM-5.3 as “the most cyber-capable open-weight model” to date, lagging US frontier models by about four months. Researchers successfully used GLM-5.3 to discover and chain together previously unknown vulnerabilities in a web browser, demonstrating its potential for real-world exploitation.

AWS blog · 2026-09-29

How Condé Nast built multimodal video discovery with Amazon Bedrock

  • Condé Nast partnered with AWS to build a multimodal video discovery solution using Amazon Bedrock and Amazon OpenSearch Service, reducing video discovery time from 250 minutes to under 2 minutes.
  • The solution leverages the TwelveLabs Marengo embedding model, accessed through Amazon Bedrock, to jointly encode visual, audio, and transcript signals for semantic search.
  • A decoupled architecture separates ingestion (embedding generation via Marengo and indexing into OpenSearch) from serving (querying and returning timestamped results), enabling scalability and resilience.
  • The system achieves high availability through multi-AZ deployment of Amazon OpenSearch Service, Amazon DocumentDB, and compute tiers utilizing Auto Scaling groups, resulting in an estimated $800,000 in annual operational savings.

Cloudera and Mistral Partner to Bring Specialized, Sovereign Intelligence to Enterprise Data

Mistral AI and Cloudera have partnered to offer enterprises greater control over their AI deployments. The core offering focuses on enabling inference and custom model training directly within a customer’s existing data environment—whether on-premise, in a private cloud, public cloud, or even air-gapped systems. This addresses the need for “sovereign AI,” where data, compute, and operations remain fully under customer control.

Mistral’s models will integrate with Cloudera’s hybrid data platform, allowing organizations to run AI without transferring sensitive data. Crucially, enterprises can leverage decades of proprietary data—such as loan decisions or production telemetry—to train customized AI models. This process generates AI tailored to specific business needs, with the company retaining full ownership of both the data and the resulting intelligence.

The partnership emphasizes a shift from relying on generic, rented AI models to owning specialized intelligence built on an organization’s unique data assets. Cloudera manages 30 exabytes of customer data, providing a substantial base for these customized AI applications.
