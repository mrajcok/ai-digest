OpenAI’s math solutions aren’t meeting the field’s standards yet

OpenAI recently released claimed solutions to difficult math problems, but their adherence to community standards is being questioned.

  • OpenAI released 719 manuscripts detailing claimed solutions, but only 10 included the model’s chain of thought.
  • Only 42% of the released proofs had been formally verified – a process mathematicians recommend to ensure rigor.
  • A paper from the University of Cambridge and King’s College London identified discrepancies between the “natural language” explanation and the formal code (Lean) for a solution related to Navier-Stokes equations.
  • The Advisory Group on Mathematics and Artificial Intelligence (AGMAI) has asked OpenAI to stop testing on proprietary models and to fund human mathematicians to validate their findings, recommendations not fully addressed in this release.

Musubi released PolicyLM-1.7B, an open-weight decision model designed for real-time content moderation.

  • The model aims to apply content policies, written in plain English, to messages in under 50 milliseconds.
  • PolicyLM-1.7B outputs a binary judgement—content either falls into a specified category or does not—and is designed to match the speed and cost of existing AI classifiers.
