The sameness problem behind those unappetizing AI-generated menus

AI-generated menus are exhibiting a “sameness” problem resulting in unnaturally flawless and unsettling food imagery.

  • Large language models (LLMs) and diffusion models (like those powering ChatGPT and Midjourney) are trained on datasets that often prioritize “pleasingness” and may include AI-generated content, leading to homogenization of outputs.
  • Repeated edits to AI-generated menus can exacerbate the problem, resulting in increasingly smooth and unrealistic food depictions.
  • The issue may stem from “convergence,” a degradation of output quality that isn’t complete model collapse, but where AI models rely heavily on existing menu styles (e.g., Chili’s from 2015).
  • Research from the University of Duisburg-Essen suggests these images trigger an “uncanny valley” effect, eliciting disgust and unease due to their almost-real but distinctly artificial appearance.

OpenAI’s new reasoning technique alarms AI safety experts

OpenAI’s upcoming “Astra” model will utilize a new reasoning technique called “recurrent depth” or “opaque recurrence.”

  • The technique allows the model to move away from sequential reasoning, processing queries in loops.
  • This approach reportedly makes the model’s reasoning process harder to monitor via traditional “chain of thought” (CoT) logging.
  • AI safety experts, including those at Redwood Research and Anthropic, have voiced concerns that wider adoption of opaque recurrence could severely limit AI monitorability and potentially lead to a “race to the bottom” in safety practices.
  • OpenAI maintains a commitment to CoT monitoring and states Astra’s usage of the technique is currently limited; however, other labs like Google DeepMind are also discussing the technique.
