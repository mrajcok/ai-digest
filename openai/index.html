OpenAI and Cisco introduce Shirudo

OpenAI and Cisco have announced Shirudo, an open-source runtime designed to protect running Linux systems without requiring restarts during vulnerability patching. A test demonstrated Shirudo – using a shield generated by OpenAI’s Codex – blocking a Java4Shell exploit against a running Elasticsearch service.

The system leverages Linux’s eBPF technology to apply targeted security protections by observing system activity and utilizes cryptographic signing and TPM hardware verification for trusted deployment. Shirudo is built around live protection, defense at machine speed via programmable agents, and verifiable deployment.

OpenAI and Cisco intend Shirudo as a first step towards adaptable infrastructure, aiming to use AI agents to quickly identify vulnerabilities and deploy eBPF shields, ultimately minimizing disruption and improving security. The project is available on GitHub under a BSD 2-Clause license, with BPF programs dually licensed under BSD and GPL.

Perplexity and Intelligence

OpenAI’s Chief Scientist, Jakub Pachocki, argues that tracking perplexity – or cross-entropy loss – is key to understanding AI progress and establishing shared safety standards. He proposes that advancements in “intelligence substrate” are primarily driven by improving the ability of models to compress and predict data, as measured by perplexity. GPT-2 achieved state-of-the-art perplexity and coherent paragraph generation, GPT-3.5 reached ~0.99 log-perplexity enabling fluent conversation (and forming the basis for ChatGPT), GPT-4 reached ~0.91 and productive chain-of-thought reasoning, and current models below 0.8 exhibit transformative abilities in areas like cybersecurity and mathematics.

Pachocki notes a potential acceleration in progress due to AI contributing to algorithmic research, citing examples of models solving long-standing mathematical problems at ~0.76 log-perplexity. He suggests that a log-perplexity between ~0.68-0.7 may unlock further transformative capabilities. He advocates for industry-wide transparency in perplexity measurements and proposes establishing development limits tied to perplexity, pausing advancement until alignment and monitoring capabilities improve. The post acknowledges challenges in using perplexity as a universal metric, including variations in datasets and the fact that it doesn’t directly correlate to specific task performance.

Can AI help scientists identify promising drug candidates?

OpenAI has released MedChemBench, a benchmark for evaluating AI models on reasoning tasks specific to medicinal chemistry. It assesses a model’s ability to connect molecular structures with experimental evidence to explain results, compare compounds, and predict properties – crucial for drug discovery where optimizing one trait can negatively impact others. The benchmark consists of 99 problems drawn from 117 published papers and patents, testing molecular reconstruction, scientific reasoning, compound selection, and property prediction.

Across the highest reasoning setting (“xhigh”), GPT‑6.2 Astra achieved 84.5% of available credit, surpassing GPT‑6 Astra (83.3%), GPT‑6.1 Sol (82.0%), and GPT‑5.6 Sol (58.2%). Astra demonstrated particular strength in reconstructing molecular structures (95.4% credit) and explaining evidence. However, challenges remain in compound selection and numerical property prediction. The benchmark results do not demonstrate drug discovery capabilities, but rather diagnose areas for improvement in AI reasoning within the field.

Combatting adversarial distillation

OpenAI announced strengthened safeguards against “adversarial distillation,” a technique where bad actors reproduce a model’s capabilities without its safety protections. This is achieved by training a new model on the outputs – and crucially, the reasoning traces – of a more advanced, safeguarded model like GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol. OpenAI found that access to these detailed reasoning traces significantly increased the transfer of capabilities, particularly in cybersecurity, even when the underlying task was permissible.

To combat this, OpenAI implemented defenses at both the individual request level – through adversarial training, classifiers, and API restrictions – and at the campaign level, detecting coordinated extraction attempts. Internal testing shows these updates drastically reduced attack success rates; specifically, recovering protected reasoning fell from 54.0% to 0.2% with GPT-6.2 Astra.

OpenAI is also investigating and disrupting ongoing distillation campaigns, sharing findings with industry partners and advocating for legislation to address national security implications, including identifying and sanctioning foreign actors attempting illicit capability extraction. They encourage other developers to protect reasoning traces, log attacks for regression testing, and coordinate safeguards across the industry.

How OpenAI’s Performance Factory keeps ChatGPT fast

OpenAI has developed the “Performance Factory,” an automated system leveraging Codex to identify and resolve performance regressions in services like ChatGPT. Traditionally, performance engineering required intensive manual investigation of metrics, logs, and code. The Performance Factory streamlines this process through automated workflows.

The core workflow, the “Regression Workflow,” automatically investigates slowdowns triggered by performance alerts, proposes fixes, and verifies recovery – handling over 1,700 investigations and 600 fixes in three months, equivalent to 17 automated investigations daily. This system consists of a task tracker with configurable workflows, a dispatch loop assigning work to agents, a cloud API for running Codex, and subscriptions that trigger actions based on events (like code deployments).

To ensure accuracy, the system incorporates adversarial review – a separate Codex session challenging proposed diagnoses and fixes. OpenAI emphasizes the importance of evaluating agent behavior and continuously improving workflows through testing and monitoring, aiming to proactively identify and address performance issues even before alerts fire. They are working towards agent-driven optimizations and even self-repair capabilities.
