Researchers use AI to design proteins that cut up other proteins

Researchers at the University of Washington, led by Nobel laureate David Baker, used AI to design novel proteins capable of cleaving other proteins.

  • The research utilized RFdiffusion2 to generate protein backbones constrained to accommodate key catalytic amino acids and target sequences.
  • ProteinMPNN populated these backbones with specific amino acids, optimizing for structural compatibility and catalytic site formation.
  • Both cysteine and zinc protease designs demonstrated successful protein cleavage, with activity levels initially lower than natural enzymes, but significantly improved through iterative mutation and refinement (up to 10^9 and 10^10 acceleration of peptide bond cleavage).
  • The designed proteases were successfully targeted towards proteins associated with neurological diseases like Alzheimer’s and frontotemporal dementia, demonstrating potential for therapeutic applications, though delivery and immune response challenges remain.

AI coding agents generate more code, but not more software

AI coding agents increase code generation but don’t demonstrably improve software output, according to a Harvard University study reported by Ars Technica.

  • The study, using data from Jellyfish encompassing over 700 firms and 300 million “work events” from 2021 through March 2026, found AI coding agents increased total lines of code generated by 30%, commits by 20%, and pull requests by 23%.
  • Despite these increases, there was no statistically significant change in the rate of issue or epic resolution (software feature completion) tracked by tools like Jira.
  • The average code review process increased by 49% after the introduction of AI agents, with pull requests requiring changes nearly doubling and comments per pull request increasing by 35%.
  • The study found no significant changes in overall employment at the firms studied after AI agent introduction and also noted AI agents accounted for only 23.3% of review comments and 10.8% of pull request resolutions as of March 2026.
