DeepMind’s hurricane breakthrough has surprised weather scientists

DeepMind and Google Research have developed WeatherNext, an AI model for improved cyclone forecasting.

  • WeatherNext demonstrated an 80% confidence in predicting Hurricane Melissa (October 2025) would hit Jamaica as a Category 5 hurricane five days before landfall.
  • The model provides, on average, one day more lead time in forecasts compared to existing models—meaning three-day-out predictions are as accurate as previous models’ two-day-out predictions.
  • WeatherNext was trained on both general weather data and cyclone data to overcome the challenge of limited extreme event training data.
  • The model currently generates 1,000 potential storm scenarios, a scale not feasible with existing numerical models, and DeepMind is open-sourcing the models used during hurricane season for further research.

Large genome models used to design new viruses

Large genome models were used to design viable viruses.

  • Stanford University researchers used large genome models, Evo 1 and Evo 2, trained on DNA sequences to generate new viral genomes related to the ΦX174 virus, which infects E. coli.
  • The models were provided with over 2 million bases of DNA from bacteriophages and fine-tuned on the Microviridae family, then prompted with short sequences from ΦX174 to generate complete genome sequences.
  • Of 285 synthesized viral sequences, 16 inhibited E. coli growth, demonstrating functionality, and a cocktail of AI-designed viruses outperformed a natural bacteriophage cocktail in overcoming bacterial resistance.
  • Researchers caution that while current models were trained without vertebrate virus sequences, future models could be used to design viruses targeting vertebrates, calling for better governance and regulation of AI in this field.
