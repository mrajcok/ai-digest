AI was supposed to hit new grads hard. So far, unemployment data says otherwise.

A recent working paper from CESifo researchers finds no evidence of significant displacement or reduced hiring of recent college graduates due to AI.

  • The study analyzed US Census Bureau Current Population Survey data from 2022 to 2026, focusing on unemployment trends among Bachelor’s degree recipients aged 22-25.
  • Summer 2026 unemployment for recent graduates (7.3%) remained within the typical range (6.3% - 7.8%) observed in prior years.
  • Researchers compared recent graduates to non-college graduates and older college graduates, as well as analyzing employment by "AI exposure" based on a 2023 study, finding no statistically significant trends.
  • The study acknowledges that future graduating classes (2027 and beyond) may be more affected as AI use intensifies, and further data is needed to assess long-term impacts.
